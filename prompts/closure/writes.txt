Write two things:

(a) **`bug_db/bugs.json`** -- for each observation you closed, add these fields
    to its entry and change nothing else about it:

      "closed_on":     "{when}",
      "closed_commit": "<the full cvc5 sha>",
      "closed_pr":     "https://github.com/cvc5/cvc5/pull/<n>",
      "closed_why":    "<what the commit changed, and what you re-read to
                        confirm the claim is now false>"

    cvc5 squashes with the pull request number in the subject, as `(#12345)`;
    take it from there. Omit `closed_pr` entirely if the subject names none --
    do not guess a number. Keep every existing field and value exactly as it
    is; do not reorder, rewrite or delete entries, or touch `bug_db/runs/`.
    A later analyzer run that re-observes a closed identity moves `last_seen`
    past `closed_on`, and Koine reports a reopen candidate. That contradiction
    is how a wrong closure surfaces; do not tidy it away.

(b) **`docs/experience.md`** -- one section per cvc5 pull request that closed
    something, in the order and shape that file sets out. At a high level,
    explain what was wrong, what the change did about it, and what that says
    about the check that found it. Record whether the change cites us, without
    arguing for attribution: a fix cvc5 found independently still closed an
    observation of ours and is evidence the check pointed at something real.
